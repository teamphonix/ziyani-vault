# Candle Terms and Process

This is the beginner foundation. Jocelyn should study these terms until she can explain them in plain language.

## Standard Build

The standard build is the locked formula for one candle:

- jar size
- wax type
- wick type and size
- fragrance load
- label dimensions
- safety label plan

Do not change all of these at once. If something goes wrong, she will not know what caused it.

## Wax

Wax is the body of the candle. It holds the fragrance, controls the burn, and affects appearance.

Common wax paths:

- soy wax
- coconut soy blend
- paraffin blend
- beeswax blend

Every wax has different temperatures, cure times, and wick needs.

## Wick

The wick is the engine. It controls flame size, melt pool, heat, scent throw, soot, and safety.

If the wick is too small:

- tunneling can happen
- scent throw can be weak
- wax can remain on the sides

If the wick is too large:

- flame can be too high
- jar can overheat
- soot can appear
- candle can burn too fast

## Fragrance Load

Fragrance load is how much oil goes into the wax. It is measured by weight, usually as a percentage of wax weight.

Example:

- 300g wax
- 8 percent fragrance load
- 24g fragrance oil

For Ziyani, base and accent oils must be measured and recorded.

## Cold Throw

Cold throw is how strong the candle smells before it is lit.

## Hot Throw

Hot throw is how strong the candle smells while it is burning.

A fragrance can smell amazing in the bottle but perform badly in wax, so hot throw must be tested.

## Melt Pool

The melt pool is the liquid wax area created while the candle burns.

Watch how far the melt pool reaches, how deep it gets, and whether the jar becomes too hot.

## Tunneling

Tunneling happens when the candle burns down the center and leaves wax stuck on the sides.

Common causes:

- wick too small
- first burn too short
- wax/wick mismatch

## Cure Time

Cure time is the resting time after pouring. It gives wax and fragrance time to bind.

Do not judge final scent performance too early.

## Batch Record

Every batch should record:

- date
- candle name
- vessel
- wax type
- wax grams
- base fragrance grams
- accent fragrance grams
- wick size
- melt temperature
- fragrance temperature
- pour temperature
- cure date
- cold throw
- hot throw
- burn notes
- label notes

## Burn Testing

Burn testing proves whether a candle is sellable.

Track:

- flame height
- melt pool
- soot
- wick mushrooming
- tunneling
- jar temperature
- cold throw
- hot throw
- surface after cooling

Never sell an untested candle.
